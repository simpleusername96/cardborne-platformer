# Vehicle Stage One Future Directions

This document is deliberately not an active implementation plan. It records what later work could explore without expanding the Stage 1 pull request.

## Lessons the Stage 1 build is designed to validate

These statements become validated only when the linked report contains passing evidence and direct play observations:

- Direct strafe movement plus independent aim is a clear low-production-cost vehicle control model.
- Manual target priority remains meaningful when fixed turrets, generators, mines, and boss pylons materially change pressure.
- A passive seeker can reduce cleanup without taking ownership of the dangerous target decision.
- Dash becomes a stronger identity when it is dependable defensively and can be converted into a trail or impact weapon.
- One authored continuous field can create rhythm through safe deployment, open pressure, installation routing, optional risk, a card pause, and a locked boss payoff.
- Behavior-changing cards are understandable when their effect is visible immediately in the next fight.
- A compact garage can support replay and minimal unlocks without hiding a weak combat loop behind errands or economy.

The final report must mark any failed statement plainly. A passing automated validator alone is not evidence that the control loop is enjoyable.

## Speculative connected stages and themes

Later authored stages could connect the flooded foundry to a tidal archive, broken aqueduct, submerged quarry, storm refinery, or ancient drydock. Each should introduce a new spatial problem, not merely recolor the current map. Connections could support shortcuts and revisits only after the authored single-stage route proves worth extending.

## Speculative exploration and puzzle systems

Deeper exploration could use water-level controls, movable floodgates, current-routing machinery, line-of-sight relay puzzles, breakable sightline blockers, timed extraction, and vehicle tools that serve both combat and navigation. Loose salvage and crates are supporting texture, not a substitute for a puzzle or discovery system.

## Speculative vehicle archetypes

Possible later chassis include a heavy ram crawler, precise long-range skiff, drone carrier, volatile overheat interceptor, or barrier-focused salvage rig. New vehicles should change movement, preferred range, and upgrade interactions. Cosmetic silhouettes with identical positioning decisions are not distinct archetypes.

## Speculative enemy and installation roles

Future roles could include mobile shield escorts, repair convoys, artillery spotters, projectile-interception towers, tether nodes, current generators, decoy emitters, armored gates, destructible cover fabricators, and enemies that steal or corrupt field pickups. Each role needs a clear target-priority consequence and a readable counter.

## Speculative field bosses and stage bosses

Additional field bosses could guard shortcuts or rare modules and remain escapable. Stage bosses could test line-of-sight manipulation, moving cover, escort denial, destructible armor, coupled generator networks, or arena hazards controlled by priority targets. Later phases should recombine taught rules instead of replacing the combat language with unrelated spectacle.

## Speculative garage/base evolution

The garage could later add vehicle comparison, module slots, build presets, a codex, route selection, test firing, and visual damage repair. A walkable base should be considered only when movement through it creates worthwhile decisions; NPC errands, long corridors, and repair costs that block replay remain poor fits.

## Speculative equipment, resources, repair, and progression

Persistent progression could unlock sidegrades, new card families, alternate primaries, passive systems, active skills, and route options. Resources should answer a clear decision such as unlocking a new option or repairing between linked stages. Raw permanent power should not become a mandatory grind that invalidates encounter tuning.

## Speculative upgrade pools and synergy families

A larger pool could grow around five readable families: projectile geometry, seeker/drone command, dash collision, EMP/control, and pickup conversion. Synergies should be discoverable through consistent triggers and avoid screen-filling proc chains that hide incoming damage. Tradeoffs—range for spread, cadence for heat, defense for speed—could create stronger authored machines than small universal bonuses.

## Speculative accessibility and controls

Later options could include aim assist strength, auto-fire, hold/toggle fire, dash direction policy, right-stick dead-zone tuning, projectile contrast, telegraph intensity, camera shake, screen flash, reduced motion, damage-source recap, remapping, and alternative color/shape channels. Assistance should preserve target priority and positioning decisions.

## Speculative audio and visual polish

A production pass could replace generated waveforms with licensed or commissioned weapon, impact, engine, warning, and environment audio; add bespoke raster vehicles and bosses; use restrained surface art; improve directional impact and damage states; and add music that responds to installation, elite, and boss pressure. Threat telegraphs must remain visually dominant over atmosphere.

## Speculative procedural variation

Procedural encounter or route variation should follow—not precede—evidence that the authored stage remains satisfying across repeated runs. A safe first step would vary a few authored spawn groups, card offers, pickup positions, and optional branch rewards while preserving tested sightlines, cover, route width, and boss readability.
